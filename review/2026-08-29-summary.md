# The night the room became a photograph — 29.8.2026

**Two minutes. The one thing to look at first is at the bottom.**

---

## What changed

**The door now stands in a real entrance.** The wall, the floor, the two lit
sconces and the olive tree are your photograph; the door, its frame and its
shadows are still the live drawing, composited on top. Every colour, every
size, every handle still works exactly as before — the room is the only thing
that became real.

**The photograph is never inside the drawing**, and that one decision is what
makes the rest safe:

- `?bare=1` — what every measuring tool in the repo photographs — still gets a
  pure drawing. **All 52 comparison sheets came back byte-identical.**
- If `assets/room.webp` is missing, blocked, or slow, the door stands in the
  drawn room it has always had. No error, no gap, nothing to notice. There is
  an audit route that blocks the file on purpose and requires the page to come
  up normal.
- The photograph's own floor line lands on the line the door stands on, at
  every screen size, **within 0.3–1.7 px** — measured in pixels on the real
  page, not computed from the same numbers that placed it.

**The site is four files now, not three,** and the README says which one you
can delete: `assets/room.webp`, 82 KB. The other three are the site.

---

## What I refused, and why

**I did not warm the door to match the room.** It was built — it is the
physically correct move and the spec asked for it — and then measured against
your manufacturer's own colour chart:

| colour | before | with the warming |
|---|---|---|
| לבן (9016D) | dead on | **renders as beige** |
| קרם (9001D) | dead on | noticeably off |

A customer choosing white would have been shown beige. So the door keeps its
measured colour and **the photograph was graded to meet it instead** — and how
far to grade came from your own photographs: I measured door-brightness against
wall-brightness on 47 installed doors and matched that ratio.

**I did not add the satin sheen.** Built twice in a previous round, measured
worse twice. The photograph raises the bar on the leaf but does not change that
argument.

**I did not make the door bigger on a desktop.** It is 2100 mm standing against
a 3.3 m photographed wall — that IS its size in that room. Making it larger
would be the one dishonest thing in an otherwise honest picture.

## What is better on a phone

At **320 px** — the small screen, not the one people test on — **every step
used to show a question with no answers at all.** Rail, door, question,
explanation, price bar: 607 px of content on a 568 px screen. Now every step
shows options.

The cost was the door: 318 px tall down to 250 on that screen. It is the trade
the brief asked for — the answer has to be with the question.

**One case is still short and I have not hidden it:** the very first screen at
320 px arrives with the size tiles 78 px below the fold. Every other step is
fine. Closing it would mean moving the "this is an illustration" note out from
under the drawing, which is a promise you made and not mine to move.

---

## The instruments

Three new checks, and one of them found something the same night:

- **an answer is on screen with the question**, every step, every viewport
- **the photographed floor is the floor the door stands on**, measured in pixels
- **the accent is spent only where it means "this one is chosen"** — asked for
  in the design brief months ago, never built until now. I broke it on purpose
  to watch it fire.

Nothing was weakened to go green.
