Dlatot Magen · first-time-user review

Nine icons, no words, and the send button off the bottom of the screen.

I opened the live site knowing nothing about it and tried to buy a door. The craft on display is real — this is a handsome page. But the navigation refuses to tell me where I am, and on a 1280×720 laptop the flow ends with its own call to action below the fold. Everything below was measured in the browser, not eyeballed.

30 August 2026 head c829ea3 (agent run 58) 1440×900 · 1280×720 · 1920×1080

First, what is genuinely good

  • The render is beautiful. Wall lights, a plant, cast shadows, a real room. It looks like a premium product rather than a diagram, and it is the reason I trusted the page at all.
  • The honesty line under the image — הציור באתר הוא הדמיה ממוחשבת — sets expectations before anyone can feel misled.
  • "Start from a door we already installed · 30 real doors" is exactly the right front door for someone with no vocabulary for this. It is the single best idea on the page.
  • The price is always on screen and moves as you choose.
  • Three languages (he/en/ru) — right for this market, and rarely bothered with.
  • The summary step is a clean, icon-led spec table. That page is doing its job.
  • Undo/redo start correctly disabled — someone thought about the empty state.

The problems are not taste. Every one below is a place where the interface knows something and declines to tell me.

What went wrong, in the order I hit it

Blocking the step rail

Nine circular icons, none of them labelled, and only six fit

The whole navigation is nine unlabelled glyphs. Every one carries a perfectly good aria-label — מבנה הדלת, משקוף, צבע, ידית משיכה, מנעול, פרזול, עיצוב החזית, חלון, סיכום — which a screen reader gets and my eyes do not. Zero of the nine render any visible text.

And the rail is a horizontal scroller: the track is 310 px wide holding 428 px of icons, so three of the nine steps are off-screen at every desktop size I tried. There is an affordance — an 18 px mask-image fade at each end, added deliberately for exactly this complaint — but at 18 px against a 44 px circle it reads as "this icon is clipped," not as "there are three more questions this way." I could not tell how long this was going to take.

And the obvious fix has already been tried and measured out. js/app.js records why the labels went: the step NUMBER duplicated the NN / 08 counter and cost 24 px of a phone's most expensive strip, and the TITLE only ever showed above 1100 px, where nine names in a 310 px column measured scrollWidth 429 — nine truncated words. So "just label them" is not available in one row. Nine circles at the 44 px tap floor need 428 px and the card is capped at 360. The recommendation below has to break that constraint rather than ignore it.

The one text cue, 08 / 01, makes it worse. Glanced at, it reads as "8 of 1". It is RTL for "1 of 8" — but the rail holds nine icons, so the counter and the navigation disagree about how big this is.

Fix — the one option the measurements have not already ruled out

Let the rail wrap to two rows instead of scrolling. Five icons plus four is roughly 5 × 64 px = 320 px per row, which fits the 360 px card where one row of nine (428 px) never can — and a wrapped rail shows all nine at once, so it needs no fade and no scroll affordance at all. That also buys back the room for a short label under each circle, which is the thing that was measured out only because one row could not hold it. Measure the two-row height against the panel budget before committing: this trades horizontal overflow for vertical, and vertical is the axis the panel is already short of. Then make NN / 08 agree with the rail, which holds nine.

Blocking summary step · 1280×720

I reached the end and the send button was not on screen

On the final step the choices panel holds 593 px more content than it can show at 1280×720. The main call to action — שלחו את הדלת בוואטסאפ, 232×52, the entire purpose of the site — sits below that fold. The action bar at the panel's foot, which showed הבא on every previous step, now shows only הקודם. Nothing tells me to scroll.

Fix

On the summary step, the pinned action bar should carry the WhatsApp send where הבא lives everywhere else. The bar is already pinned and already the place the eye goes after each step; it should not go empty on the one step that matters.

Costs real money step 1 · 1280×720

The most expensive question on the site is pre-answered and half off-screen

Step 1 ends with כיוון פתיחה — which way the door opens. At 1280×720 those two radio buttons are the only two controls in the whole page that the viewport cuts through; I measured for clipped controls and got exactly ימין, פנימה and שמאל, פנימה. They already have a default selected. So the obvious move — glance, hit הבא — silently accepts a handing I never chose.

This project's own CLAUDE.md calls the ימין/שמאל convention "the only mistake on the list that costs real money." It is currently the easiest thing on the page to skip.

Fix

Handing should not have a silent default. Either leave it genuinely unselected and require a choice before הבא enables, or promote it to its own confirm-row on the summary — "opens right, inward — is that right?" — so it is impossible to send an order without having looked at it once.

Structural desktop layout

Three quarters of the screen is a picture that barely changes

At 1440 the door image takes roughly 73% of the width and the choices — the thing I am actually here to do — get a narrow column that scrolls internally on every single step: 113 px hidden on arrival, up to 599 px mid-flow. Meanwhile the image is nearly identical between steps; picking a lock swaps a small piece of hardware I have to hunt for.

For a configurator the proportions are inverted. The picture is the reward; the choosing is the task, and the task has been given the smaller, more awkward half.

Fix

Widen the choices column on large screens — the door does not need 1,060 px to be convincing — so a step's options fit without internal scrolling. Where a step genuinely overflows, the panel needs a visible scroll cue. And when a choice changes a small detail, briefly draw attention to the part that changed; otherwise the live preview, which is the page's main selling point, goes unnoticed.

Trust arrival state

It offers to send my order before I have chosen anything

Two WhatsApp buttons are live on arrival, on a door I have not touched. A confused first-timer can fire off a default door as though it were a considered order — and from Peretz's side that is indistinguishable from a real one.

Fix

Before any choice is made, the floating button should ask a question rather than submit an order — יש לי שאלה — and become שלחו את הדלת once the customer has actually chosen something. Same channel, honest label.

Polish throughout

Four smaller things that each cost a second of confusion

Withdrawn — this was my instrument's fault, not the page's. I reported that the step heading renders inside a gold box that reads as a text input. It does not, for a real user. The stylesheet already has .sect__title:focus { outline: none } with the ring only on :focus-visible — correct. My probe drove the page with a scripted el.click(), which dispatches no trusted pointer event, so the browser attributed goStep's programmatic focus to the keyboard and drew the ring. Re-tested three ways: real mouse click → outline: none; real keyboard → ring, as it should; scripted click → ring, which is what I saw. The page was right and the harness was wrong.

Two unlabelled grey circles sit on the artwork. Undo and redo, correctly disabled at the start, so they are two dead buttons with no text explaining themselves — on top of the photograph, next to the language switcher, which is also on the photograph. Page chrome floating over the product shot makes the shot look like a background rather than the thing being sold.

Six size tiles, six near-identical door glyphs. The tiles differ only in their text; the pictures are the same rectangle at the same size, so they add nothing and slow the scan. Either draw them to relative proportion — which is the actual difference — or drop the glyph and let the numbers do the work.

Things are said twice. נמדוד אצלכם במדויק — בחינם appears in the step description and again under the size grid. ₪3,195 appears on the floating card and on the selected tile. Neither is wrong; both make a dense panel denser.

What I would change, in order

01 Put the send button in the action bar on the summary step. The flow currently ends in an empty bar with the conversion below the fold. small
02 Wrap the step rail to two rows and label the icons. Two rows fit the 360 px card where one row of nine cannot — the constraint that forced the labels out. medium
03 Make handing an explicit choice. No silent default on the one decision the project itself says costs real money. small
04 Give the panel a visible scroll cue wherever a step overflows — every step overflows at 1280×720. small
05 Rebalance the desktop split so the choices column is wide enough that most steps need no internal scrolling at all. medium
06 Relabel the send button before any choice is made, and lift undo/redo and the language switcher off the photograph. small

Limits

This is desktop only, in Chromium, at three viewport sizes, on the committed build at c829ea3 — not the deployed GitHub Pages copy, though the two should match. I judged the Hebrew interface; I did not walk the English or Russian versions, and RTL layout bugs often live in exactly that gap. I also did not test with a keyboard or a screen reader here — worth noting because several findings above (unlabelled icons that do carry aria-labels, disabled undo buttons) suggest the accessibility layer may be in better shape than the visual one, which is an unusual and fixable asymmetry.

Probes, all gitignored under tools/_*.mjs: _uxshots (arrival state and visible-control inventory), _uxprobe (clipped controls, rail metrics, panel overflow), _uxflow (walking the eight steps), _uxsummary (rail scroll geometry and final-step CTA position). No source file was changed.
