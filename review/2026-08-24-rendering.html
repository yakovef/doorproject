Dlatot Magen · rendering study

Detail is nearly free. Structure is what costs.

You asked whether there is a better way to draw the door and its accessories, in more detail. There is — but not where I expected, and not where the repo has been looking. Every technique below was measured on this drawing at 6× CPU throttle before it was written down, and two things the project records as settled turn out to be wrong.

24 August 2026 web research + 7 local probes Chromium 1194 · 390×844 · 6× throttle

The answer, first

The project has been treating realism and performance as the same constraint. They are not. I split one colour tap on the worst door into its phases:

You can buy a great deal more material realism for roughly nothing. What you cannot afford is another element. Filter cost scales with the pixel area you filter; DOM cost scales with how many things you draw — and this drawing is bottlenecked entirely on the second.

And the budget is going almost entirely to one thing. The quatrefoil grille — a column of medallions repeated down the leaf, not the plainer iron option — is 1,752 of the worst door's 2,175 elements: 81%. The whole rest of the door — leaf, frame, mouldings, threshold, hardware, shadows, glass — is 423. iron is cheap already: grillePaths' own comment calls it "ONE composition, not a repeating pattern" — five bars, a six-link chain, two end caps, none of it scaling with leaf size.

The techniques

Each scored against what you asked: how it works, how hard it is for me to build here, what it costs, and whether it fits.

SVG <pattern> for repeating ornament

adopt

Declare the motif once; the browser tiles it at raster time. One element in the DOM regardless of how many repeats appear.

EffortMedium — per grille family
Measured144 motifs: 3,026 el / 100 ms → 26 el / 3 ms
ConstraintsAll clear
FitAimed straight at the 81%

33× cheaper at identical visual density, and it strands nothing — verified by reading collide.mjs directly: it queries only [data-hw], [data-mount], [data-pane], [data-detail], [data-relight] and [data-hitpad], and rules.js only ever asks whether a grille exists, never its shape — neither reads an individual grille member's box.

Corrected after reading js/renderer.js line by line: not every grille family is a target. mesh (etched glass) already ships as a <pattern> — its own comment says why: "twelve by fifty-odd cells drawn one at a time is thousands of elements... this is a texture." grid, scroll, deco and arch are already cheap: a handful of lines or one or two hand-placed motifs, none of it scaling with leaf size. iron is explicitly documented in the code as "ONE composition, not a repeating pattern." The one real target in the applied-ironwork family is quatrefoil — a column of n medallions whose count grows with leaf height, each one costing roughly thirty elements. That is the family behind the 81% figure above, and the only one this technique needs to touch.

In-place attribute updates for a recolour

adopt

Stop rebuilding the tree when only the paint changed. Rewrite the colour-bearing attributes on the nodes already there.

EffortMedium
Measured149 ms → 9 ms on the worst door
ConstraintsAll clear
FitThe commonest tap on the page

This is the single biggest win available, and the obvious version of it is wrong. I measured all three strategies:

Strategytoucheddefaultworst
rebuild via innerHTML (ships today)2,13017.4 ms149.0 ms
rewrite stops & fills in place4932.1 ms9.0 ms
one CSS custom property on the root14.6 ms33.0 ms

The custom-property approach — which is what the research proposed, and what I proposed in my own review last week — is 3.7× slower than just writing the attributes, because changing a property on the root invalidates style for all 2,130 descendants. Worth knowing before building the elegant version.

feTurbulence → feDisplacementMap

viable

Procedural noise warps the pixels of whatever sits under it — surface irregularity in painted steel, the grain direction in brushed metal. No image asset of any kind.

EffortLow — it is markup
Measured−3 ms over the whole leaf (noise)
ConstraintsClears file:// outright
FitGood, with one caution

Two mechanics that will bite if unknown: the displacement bound is ±scale/2, not ±scale; and the filter region clips displaced content, so the region has to be grown by scale/2 or the edges are cut off. Also — getBBox() reports the undisplaced geometry, so this is safe on decorative surfaces and unsafe as a way to express anything rules.js must reason about.

feSpecularLighting / feDiffuseLighting

cheap, but not needed

A real Phong light. It reads the input's alpha as a height field and computes the highlight — the two-parameter physical point light that beat sixteen hand-tuned constants in the rejected 3D experiment, available inside SVG with no second renderer.

EffortMedium
Measured+7 ms over the whole leaf
ConstraintsAll clear
FitWeakest of the three

It outputs a specular map, not a finished surface — it must be composited back with feComposite operator="arithmetic". And each feSpecularLighting honours only its first light child; both Blink and WebKit silently ignore a second. But the honest objection is need, not cost: CLAUDE.md already settled that a pull bar's cross-section is a linearGradient, measured from the photograph at 28 numbers. This technique would replace an exact encoding with an approximate one.

Pre-baked texture atlas as an inline data URI

reopened

Ship real photographic material — brushed steel, paint grain — as a base64 image inside the bundle, and paint it into the drawing.

EffortHigh
CostBundle size; raster memory
ConstraintsNow clears file:// — see below
FitPossible, not obviously wise

This was recorded as dead and it is not. But the earlier evaluation also rejected raster on +36% over budget and thirteen binaries to keep fresh, and that objection is untouched by the reopening.

CSS Paint API (Houdini)

reject

A JS worklet paints a CSS background procedurally.

EffortMedium
ConstraintsRuns from file:// via a blob: URL
BlockerChromium only; drops from print
FitNo — the job is already done

Both objections raised against it are false — it does run from file://, and the worklet can be registered from a blob: URL built at runtime, so the single-bundle model survives. It still loses: not implemented in Safari or Firefox, it lives outside the SVG document so it cannot participate in getBBox, and painted CSS backgrounds are omitted from print when "background graphics" is off, which is the default — a printed order sheet would silently lose whatever it drew. That is CLAUDE.md §5's exact shape. Its only real niche is a backdrop texture, which the existing <pattern> tiles already do in about a millisecond.

DOM diffing (morphdom and similar)

reject

Patch the existing tree toward the new markup instead of replacing it.

EffortLow to adopt
CostCannot recover the parse
ConstraintsPreserves all 27 getBBox sites
FitBuys safety, not speed

It genuinely preserves retained-mode SVG and has real namespace support. But it still builds a throwaway DOM from the 278 KB string, so the 110 ms parse survives — and targeted attribute writes get to 9 ms without a dependency. Also a buildless site adding its first runtime dependency is a real cost here.

WebGL / WebGPU hybrid

reject, unchanged

Keep SVG for geometry and hit-testing; render the lit surface on the GPU beneath it.

EffortVery high
CostUnmeasured on device
ConstraintsSecond renderer, permanently
FitNo — the premise is gone

The case for this was that only a GPU could afford real lighting. That premise is now measured false: a full specular chain over the entire leaf costs about 7 ms inside plain SVG. The project's original reason for rejecting 3D — a permanent second renderer to keep in sync — stands, and the reward it was buying has evaporated.

Two things we believed that are wrong

Both verified by me directly, not taken from the research.

Canvas readback does work over file://

The repo records — and I repeated it in last week's review — that getImageData/toDataURL throw SecurityError over file://, killing every canvas design. That is too broad. Only a separate local file taints the canvas:

Source drawn into the canvasgetImageDatatoDataURL
procedural (fillRect)OKOK
data: URI PNGOKOK
data: URI SVGOKOK
blob: URLOKOK
our own inline render() outputOKOK
a separate local .png fileSecurityErrorSecurityError

Two consequences. Inline texture atlases are back on the table — and since the app must travel as three self-contained files anyway, an atlas would be a data: URI by construction, which is the case that works. More immediately: last week's "attach a PNG of the door to the WhatsApp message" idea is not https-only after all. Rasterising our own drawing works from a double-clicked index.html. I will correct that report.

Every filter we ship is running in the wrong colour space

SVG filters interpolate in linearRGB by default — unlike almost everything else in the platform — and sRGB must be opted into with color-interpolation-filters. That attribute appears nowhere in js/, css/ or index.html. Meanwhile tools/profile.mjs judges the result with 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B over raw sRGB screenshot bytes.

I took our own drift filter — the one over the leaf, the surface whose vertical falloff is the tuned model — and rendered it both ways on our own leaf colour: 43,200 channel samples differ, maximum delta 41, and mean luminance moves from 145.9 to 114.3. The four exposed filters are grain, drift, wallGrain and frost — which sit on the leaf and the pane, precisely the two surfaces with measured photographic targets. The other four are Gaussian blurs over pure black, and premultiplied black is 0 in either space, so those are unaffected.

This is not a one-line fix. The shipped falloff, glass and moulding constants were all tuned with these filters running in linearRGB. Adding the attribute moves the drawing, fails npm run profile, and shifts committed comparison sheets. It is a re-measurement against the corpus, and it should be decided deliberately — this has the same shape as the glass rebuild that hit its number and was reverted on purpose.

What I would actually do

01 Stop rebuilding the tree for a recolour. 149 ms → 9 ms, measured. Write the attributes directly; skip the custom-property version, which is slower. Guard it by asserting the patched DOM is byte-identical to a full render() of the same state, or it becomes CLAUDE.md §5 all over again.
02 Move the periodic grilles to <pattern>. 81% of the worst door's nodes, 33× cheaper. The proof it did not change the drawing is the one this repo already uses: the 110 committed sheets must come back byte-identical.
03 Then spend the freed budget on detail. Displacement on the leaf, finer moulding quirks, more ironwork members where a family is not periodic — all of it nearly free once node count is under control. This is the part of your question I can now answer yes to.
04 Decide the linearRGB question deliberately, as a re-measurement against the corpus with the sheets regenerated — not as a patch.

Limits of this study

The filter numbers are a bound, not a figure. The method forces rasterisation by screenshotting, and capture overhead sets a floor around 85 ms — so the honest statement is "filter cost is below this method's resolution", not "filter cost is zero". The ranking is sound; the absolute millisecond values are not.

One engine, no real device. Everything ran in headless Chromium 1194 on Linux x86 at 6× CPU throttle. A CPU throttle is not a mid-range Android SoC — memory bandwidth, GPU rasteriser and thermals all differ, and filter implementations differ again in WebKit and Gecko. No Safari or Firefox result appears anywhere here, including for the two reversals above, which are the most consequential claims in this report.

Run-to-run variance is real. The worst door's parse+layout measured 217 ms in one run and 149 ms in another. Treat the ratios as the finding, not the milliseconds.

Unanswered. Nothing survived verification on what rendering stack real commercial configurators actually ship, nor on OffscreenCanvas/Web Workers, WebGPU, or procedural normal-map generation for brushed metal. Read those as unanswered, not as answered in the negative.
